CASE 01

Donations and QR payments

A streamer needs to take money from two very different audiences: local wallets in the Philippines (GCash, Maya) and cards from abroad. Each audience punishes friction differently, so the page has to switch rails without a second thought from the sender.

LIVE - maxpol-ph.github.io/donate
  • One page, four rails. Card/Wise, GCash, Maya, PayPal - each with its own QR and its own instructions, so a local donor never sees a foreign form.
  • Currency switch in place. PHP and USD amount grids recalculate on the spot; the presets change with the currency instead of staying in pesos.
  • Deep links per rail. ?pay=gcash, ?pay=maya, ?pay=wise open the page already on the right tab - used for Twitch panels and per-platform links.
  • Zero backend. Static HTML + JS on GitHub Pages, QR images generated locally. Nothing to host, nothing to break, no card data anywhere near the page.
  • Distribution wired in. 9 Twitch panels, Patreon with 3 tiers, Wise link verified with 22 currencies - the page is the middle of a funnel, not an orphan.

How the demo was made (screen recordings lie)

  • Recorded from the browser, not the screen. Chrome headless + DevTools Protocol, viewport 480x900 at 2x, so every frame is exactly 960x1800 and the text stays retina-crisp.
  • Moves are code, not a shaky hand. Scroll easing, a cursor dot and tap ripples are scripted, so the capture is repeatable frame to frame.
  • Checked by machine. A verify script prints page state after each step; ffmpeg blackdetect finds no black segments in the output.
CASE 02

Content machine: one shoot, a month of posts

A four-day trip produced 282 files. Hand-posting that is weeks of work. The machine takes a folder of raw media, pairs every file with platform-specific text, and publishes on a schedule across six networks - with the queue readable and stoppable at any step.

1 inputRaw folder + post bank: 14 locations, 180 photos, 91 videos.
2 matchOne row per file in n8n Data Tables, exact filename match, 0 orphans both ways.
3 textPer-platform copy: IG 300-600, FB 400-700, Threads 200-500, TikTok 40-150, Bluesky 100-300, Shorts 60-220.
4 publish8-10 posts/day from the queue through n8n, two workflows (photo / video), launch manual.

The boring part is the value: strict filename matching means a re-named or duplicated file fails loudly instead of posting the wrong photo with the wrong caption. Duplicates and private shots are moved out of the set before anything reaches the queue.

CASE 03

Second brain: six agents, one source of truth

The problem is not "where to store notes". It is that several AI agents, each with its own profile and its own job, start inventing facts and priorities. The fix is structure plus a single hierarchy file every agent reads before touching a task.

  • One hierarchy file. Mission, money target, projects, and rules for agents in a single note: what serves the mission, what is junk, what can be delegated.
  • Six isolated agent profiles. Mentoring, inbox, farm, jobs, n8n, and a separate person's profile - separate memories, separate skills, no cross-talk.
  • 60 skills as procedures. Each recurring job is written down once (planning, agents, board rules) instead of re-derived every session.
  • Board with a mission filter. Every card carries an estimate: mission / money / support / junk. 80% of output comes from the first two, the rest is cut.
  • 1640 notes, 119 folders in a plain-Markdown vault that any tool can read - no vendor lock, no export step.
  • Scheduled work. Cron agents post, sweep and report on a timer; the human reviews the result, not the process.

Design rule I hold: the model may be wrong, the structure must not be. If an agent cannot point to the note it acted on, the note does not exist.

CASE 04

Microgreens store: one page that sells, and a mobile app behind it

A real business, not a mockup: the store front of a microgreens farm in Metro Manila. Built in 2023, before any of the AI tooling I use now - straight hand-written HTML, a catalogue, and a checkout. It still does its job: explain a product most people have never bought, state the price plainly, and take an order in two clicks. Under it sits a native ordering app for both stores, so a customer can buy from the phone instead of the browser.

LIVE - metrogreens.ph
  • One page, one decision. What it is, who it is for, what it costs, how to order - no menu, no tabs, nothing to click twice.
  • App in both stores. The same shop ships as a phone app - Google Play and App Store links sit in the footer, and the app is the same catalogue, not a stripped-down copy.
  • Price stated in public. Microgreens at a fixed per-gram price with cost per serving worked out, so the buyer compares food, not guesswork.
  • Live preview on this page. The thumbnail below is the actual site in an embedded frame - click it and you land on the real thing, not a screenshot that can go stale.
  • Static, cheap, portable. No CMS, no plugins, no monthly bill - it can move hosts in five minutes.

CASE 05

Investment round: a calculator the investor can break himself

The same farm, but the page is not selling greens - it is raising money. Round one: 10 million PHP for 20% of the company, a six-month validation round with 10 racks and about 25 kg of microgreens a day. The rule was simple: no promise on the page that the visitor cannot change himself. Every number is an input, and the whole model recalculates in the browser, including the numbers that make the deal look worse.

LIVE - manila-farm-ph.netlify.app
  • An input, not a claim. Racks, kilos, price per gram, salaries, rent, loan rate - all editable. Change one and revenue, EBITDA, cash flow and the point where cash runs out all move with it.
  • The arithmetic is visible. Revenue is kilos times price, stated line by line, so an analyst can rebuild the model in a spreadsheet instead of trusting a slide.
  • A stress test, not a best case. Six scenarios and 2,000 randomised runs; in the round-one case the farm survives 97.3% of them. The page shows the failures too.
  • Break-even in month 7. Modelled over 60 months: 33.6M PHP returned on the cheque, 3.4x, IRR 32.8% a year. Revenue 21.3M / 72.7M / 85.5M PHP over three years, EBITDA 3.8M / 24.3M / 30.3M.
  • Honest about the clock. The farms ran 2019-2023 and paused on working capital, not demand - the page says that in plain words instead of hiding it.
